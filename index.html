Brake fault finder

Brake does not stop well

  • Worn linings: renew the shoes.
  • Dirt on the lining surface: clean it.
  • Arm fitted on the wrong cam spline: refit it to the punch marks.
  • Worn cam: renew it.

Bike feels slow, brakes binding

  • Brake adjusted too tight: a warm drum after a ride is the best evidence. Re-adjust the free play.

Brake grabs when applied gently

  • Shoe ends not chamfered: chamfer them with a file.
  • Oval drum: skim it lightly on a lathe.

Brake slow or spongy to release

  • Cam binding in its housing: free it and grease it.
  • Weak shoe springs: renew them.

Source: Haynes manual no. 572, chapter 5, fault diagnosis.

Engine fault finder

Hard to start or will not start

  • Low compression: a valve held open (clearance too small), worn valve guides, wrong valve timing, worn rings or bore.
  • No spark: wet or fouled plugs, dirty contact breaker points, wrong points gap or ignition timing.
  • No fuel: blocked tank cap vent, fuel tap or pipe, or a faulty float valve.

Noisy engine

  • Tappet noise: too much valve clearance, or a weak or broken valve spring.
  • Knocking from a piston: worn piston and bore, carbon in the combustion chambers, worn gudgeon pin or small end.
  • Cam chain rattle: stretched chain or worn sprockets.

Smoking exhaust

  • Too much oil in the engine: check the level.
  • Worn bores and rings or worn valve guides.

Lack of power

  • Valves: wrong clearance, valve sticking, weak springs, poor seating or wrong valve timing.
  • Worn bores and rings.
  • Ignition: wrong timing, faulty plug caps or dirty points.

Overheating

  • Carbon build-up in the cylinder head: decoke.
  • Low oil, a faulty oil pump or a blocked oil way.
  • Ignition timing too far advanced.

Source: Haynes manual no. 572, chapter 1, fault diagnosis: engine.

Clutch and gearbox fault finder

Clutch slips: revs rise, bike does not speed up

  • No free play at the lever: re-adjust to 10–20 mm.
  • Worn friction plates: 3.0 mm new, renew the set at 2.6 mm.
  • Weak springs: 37.8 mm free length, renew at 34.7 mm.

Clutch drags: gears crunch, bike creeps with the lever pulled, neutral hard to find

  • Too much free play: re-adjust.
  • Notches worn in the drum slots or burrs on the plate tongues: dress them with a file if not too deep.

Clutch lever stiff

  • Damaged, trapped or frayed cable: renew it, check the routing has no sharp bends, and lubricate it.

Gears hard to engage

  • Bent selector forks: renew.
  • Gear clusters wrongly assembled: check the order of the pinions and the thrust washers.

Jumps out of gear

  • Worn dogs on the pinions: renew the pinions.
  • Stopper arm not seating: check its action and spring.

Gear pedal does not return

  • Broken return spring: renew it.

Source: Haynes manual no. 572, chapter 1, fault diagnosis: clutch and gearbox.

Electrical fault finder

Nothing electrical works

  • Blown fuse: find the short circuit before fitting a new 20 A fuse.
  • Battery disconnected: check the leads and look for corroded terminals.

Lights dim, horn does not work

  • Flat battery: recharge it, then check that the alternator is charging.

Bulbs keep blowing

  • Vibration or a poor earth: check the bulb holders are secure and the earth returns to the frame are clean.

Engine will not start

  • Defective ignition coil: renew it.
  • Dirty or poorly touching points: clean or renew them and reset the gap.
  • Faulty condenser: renew it.

Starts but runs erratically or misfires

  • Wrong plug gap, or worn plugs: clean and reset to 0.6–0.7 mm, or renew.
  • Wrong timing or points gap: check both.
  • Weak points arm spring, faulty condenser, cracked plug lead insulation, low alternator output.

Lacks power and overheats

  • Retarded ignition timing: check the timing and the points gap, and that the automatic timing unit has not jammed.

Source: Haynes manual no. 572, chapter 6 and chapter 3, fault diagnosis.
